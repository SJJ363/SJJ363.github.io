Everything moving in insurtech.

Funding, deals, launches and platform shifts — aggregated all day, from hundreds of outlets.

140 stories · 52 sources · updated Nov 28, 2026

What's happening

Buyers kept moving, but the targets shifted: a Dubai-based insurtech was bought to open a new regional foothold, and a global consulting firm agreed to acquire a technology advisory business. Meanwhile AI underwriting tools are being rolled out in practice, with a German broker adopting one across three lines of business and the vendor widening its European reach, and a reinsurer and a life insurer extending their own use of AI in underwriting. Funding was thinner, with about $54 million across three disclosed rounds, and one industry report put insurtech funding down 44.6% as very large rounds slowed. Cyber also drew attention, with a survey showing most insurance CEOs rank cybercrime as their top threat to growth and two big carriers mapping more than 100 accumulation scenarios.

Why it matters

Capital is flowing to deals that buy distribution and capability rather than to large new venture rounds, which favors insurtechs with working products and regional reach. As AI underwriting moves from pilots into live lines of business, watch whether it brings measurable gains in speed and accuracy, and how carriers handle the new risks it creates, including cover for AI agents themselves. On cyber, better modeling of accumulation is what will shape how much capacity insurers are willing to offer.
