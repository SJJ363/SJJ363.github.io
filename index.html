What's happening

A fresh round of deals is mostly about moving books of business rather than buying technology: Arch is taking over a dealer-services business, AIG is picking up Everest's Chile operations, and RiverStone has closed a large legacy reinsurance transfer with QBE. At the same time, AI is showing up in more specific, practical jobs, such as reviewing claims evidence at a state workers' compensation fund, tracking application status for Canada Life customers, and powering a crop insurance program from Aon and SAGE. Guidewire also launched a platform built around agentic insurance, a sign that the software vendors are packaging this work as a product.

Why it matters

The General Motors settlement over selling driver data to insurers is a reminder that the data feeding these AI and underwriting tools carries legal risk. Expect more scrutiny of where insurers get their data as it becomes central to pricing and claims. Meanwhile, the steady flow of portfolio sales suggests carriers are still pruning what they hold, so watch whether buyers pair those books with the new claims automation.
