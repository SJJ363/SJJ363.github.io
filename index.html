What's happening

The newest stories show AI moving from pilot to daily operations: one carrier now lets AI handle most first-notice-of-loss calls, and a cargo cover can be quoted straight through ChatGPT. At the same time AI is being treated as a source of risk, with AXA's survey ranking it above climate change as experts' top concern and cyber claims shifting as AI failures create new liability. Deal-making is mixed: small agency purchases like King Risk Partners adding two Florida firms continue, even as agency M&A is reported at an eight-year low with private-equity exits looming. Partnerships round out the picture, from Aviva and insureMO taking embedded insurance across Europe to Shory teaming up with the UAE's cyber council.

Why it matters

As AI takes over front-line tasks, insurers will have to decide who is accountable when an automated decision goes wrong, which is the question behind the cyber-liability shift. A slowdown in agency deals alongside looming PE exits could change who owns the brokers and agencies carriers rely on. Watch whether the thin pace of agency M&A picks up, and whether AI-specific cover keeps appearing as fast as AI-specific exposure.
