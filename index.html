What's happening

The new stories center on a gap between AI ambition and execution. A KPMG survey covered by two outlets finds insurers rating themselves AI leaders while none has rebuilt distribution around the technology. Meanwhile, practical tools keep appearing at the edges, such as one that flags gaps in medical disclosures and another that automates compliance checks for multifamily housing. Specialist capacity is also forming, with a new wildfire underwriting venture and an autonomous vehicle offering entering the US primary market. Corgi's hire of a Head of Pricing shows AI-native carriers investing in the pricing craft itself.

Why it matters

If confidence is running ahead of rebuilt processes, the advantage may go to firms that change how they sell and price rather than those that add features. Narrow, data-heavy lines like wildfire and autonomous vehicles are where new underwriting models can prove themselves first. Watch whether the next surveys and hires show distribution changing, or whether AI stays confined to back-office tasks.
