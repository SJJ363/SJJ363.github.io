What's happening

Fresh capital is flowing in smaller, more selective forms: a private investor taking a minority stake in a specialty insurer, a growth investment in an AI-minded underwriting firm, and a small European round for a claims-focused startup. Brokers keep buying too, but the targets are now niche and local, from a 107-year-old Wisconsin family agency to a Louisiana underwriter and sports insurance books. Meanwhile, AI is showing up in fraud detection and in the broker back office, even as credit analysts and insurers start asking what it means for ratings and for the physical assets that power it.

Why it matters

The pattern suggests buyers are paying for specialist expertise and distribution rather than size, which keeps pressure on independent agencies to pick a side. Watch whether ratings agencies start to treat AI adoption as a credit factor, and whether insurance capacity can keep pace with the value of data centres. Outage loss estimates that span a wide range, from $100M to $700M, show how hard it still is to price technology risk.
