What's happening

Fresh money is flowing to companies that use AI to price and select risk, led by a $75m Series B for Kalepa at a $470m valuation and a $66m raise for Soter Insure backed by Standard Chartered and SBI. At the same time, the technology is showing up on the other side of the ledger: automated tools now make 45% of special investigation unit referrals, and reporting suggests investigators haven't kept pace, while AI-sharpened attacks raise questions about whether law firms' coverage is up to date. Elsewhere, carriers are reshaping their books, with MSIG UK adding construction and marine cargo classes, Duuo leaving event and vendor insurance, and Samsung Fire, KKR and KIC moving to buy the UK insurer Canopius.

Why it matters

Investors are betting that better risk selection is where insurtech value sits, but the same automation is creating new workload and new exposures that policy wording and fraud teams have to absorb. Watch whether claims and investigation staffing adapts, and whether coverage for AI-driven losses gets clearer. A big Q3 underwriting decline at Berkshire is a reminder that even the strongest carriers are under margin pressure, which is what pushes the rest of the market toward these tools.
