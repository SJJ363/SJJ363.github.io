What's happening

Deal-making in this batch is about specialty risk rather than general distribution. Premia is buying Cowen Reinsurance from TD Bank, Protective Life is entering specialty property and casualty through a fronting carrier acquisition, and Marsh has picked up a yacht insurer that two outlets covered. Newer players are also building capacity: Vantage has launched a facultative reinsurance arm, RiverStone has entered the ILS market, and Novel has taken fresh equity from Crestview. On the technology side, the talk has turned from replacing people to equipping them, with tools aimed at MGA change requests and a ChatGPT app for travel insurance.

Why it matters

Buyers are paying for niche expertise and access to capacity, which points to more consolidation in narrow lines such as marine and high-net-worth cover. Fronting and facultative platforms are the pieces to watch, since they give new entrants a way into specialty markets. The argument that AI makes brokers more valuable, plus a survey finding that Americans reject AI in medical and coverage decisions, suggests the winners will use automation to speed up human judgment rather than replace it.
