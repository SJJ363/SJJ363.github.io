What's happening

The newest stories show AI turning into a product, a cost and a risk all at once. Cowbell, covered by two outlets, launched standalone AI liability cover for small businesses, while S&P flags AI-driven cyberattacks alongside geopolitics as a top risk for European insurers in 2027. Insurers are also quietly rebuilding their plumbing: Storebrand picked Keylane to modernize its property and casualty core, and Mutual of Omaha widened an integration with Employee Navigator. On the money side, AI-flavored fintech rounds led the headlines, and a big insurer fundraise tied to a US acquisition showed established players raising capital too.

Why it matters

As AI shows up on both sides of the ledger, as something to insure and as something to sell, insurers and their regulators have to decide how much of that risk they will carry. Marsh's push for the Fed to ease up on banks that insure AI loans shows that debate is moving into policy. Watch whether standalone AI policies spread beyond small business, and whether software spending on core systems turns into faster, cheaper service, which is a point Federato says AI alone will not fix.
