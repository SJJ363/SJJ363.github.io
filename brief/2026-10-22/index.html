The Brief

Software carve-outs and niche launches reshape insurance

Oct 22, 2026 · 140 stories · 42 sources

Big software sale talks, specialist cover launches, and cyber shutdown worries

What's happening

The new stories are a mix of dealmaking and narrow, targeted product launches. DXC Technology is reported to be in talks to sell its insurance software unit to Eldridge Industries for $2.4 bn, and smaller buyers are picking up education, health data and cyber-training businesses. At the same time, specialty cover is being sliced ever thinner, with new programs for community association liability, artisan contractors and US mid-market professions, plus fresh capacity added to a reinsurance treaty portfolio. Two disclosed rounds, including $27 million for Slingshot Insurance, total about $54 million, while Aviva and insureMO took embedded insurance across Europe and Brown & Brown unveiled an in-house intelligence platform.

Why it matters

Software owners are being reshuffled just as carriers and brokers build their own data tools, so who controls the core systems may matter more over the next year. Insurance M&A is reported down 27% in the third quarter, so a large software deal would stand out against a slower market. Also watch cyber brokers: flaws that could trigger shutdown coverage claims test how well policies handle outages, and the chief executive of Chubb is already calling for the industry to take on AI liability.
