Insurance leadership moves

264 stories · tracked since Aug 6, 2024 · latest Nov 12, 2026

Insurance leadership moves are the hirings, promotions and board appointments that change who runs an insurer, broker or insurtech. They are announced by press release and gathered in trade-press roundups.

How an appointment gets announced and read

A leadership move is a company announcing that an executive has joined, been promoted, rejoined or left a named role, from a board seat or chief executive down to a head of underwriting or a practice leader. Insurers, brokers, managing general agents, reinsurers, consultancies and regulators all issue them, usually as a press release that trade titles republish, often bundled into a weekly People Moves column. Terms a reader will meet include CUO (chief underwriting officer), practice leader, Lloyd's syndicate underwriter and lateral hire.

Why people matter so much in this industry

Insurance runs on underwriting judgement and broker relationships, so a large share of a firm's value is in its people. A senior underwriter or a producing team can bring a book of business to a rival, which is why hires from competitors are watched closely. New product lines, new countries and new technology strategies are usually signalled first by who is hired to run them. The hard part is interpretation: most announcements are routine succession, and a title alone says little about whether strategy has changed.

Who is making moves and what they signal

The announcements come from carriers, large brokers, reinsurance intermediaries, Lloyd's syndicates and specialty underwriters, with insurtech carriers such as Corgi appearing as they build out management teams. Many appointments are underwriting heads for cyber, property and casualty lines, and a noticeable group are technology and AI roles at established carriers. Moves often land beside broker acquisitions, new reinsurance units and market entries, and some feed wider debate, such as who owns and leads independent agencies.
