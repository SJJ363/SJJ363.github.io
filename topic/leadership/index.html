Insurance leadership moves

128 stories · tracked since Aug 6, 2024 · latest Aug 27, 2026

Insurance leadership moves are the appointments, promotions and departures of senior executives at insurers, brokers, reinsurers and insurtechs. Coverage ranges from a single underwriter hire to a chief-executive succession, filed individually or bundled into a weekly round-up.

Who moves, and what the announcement covers

A leadership move can be a board appointment, a C-suite hire such as chief executive, chief financial officer, chief risk officer or chief underwriting officer, or a step below that seniority, such as a regional underwriter, actuary or head of a product line. Trade outlets report each one as a short notice naming the person, the new title, the employer and sometimes the executive they succeed or the background they bring from a previous employer; several also run a recurring digest that bundles several moves from different companies into a single weekly post.

Why the pace of hiring has picked up

Insurers, reinsurers and brokers are recruiting for roles that barely existed on a traditional organisation chart: heads of AI, data-centre strategy, cyber underwriting and reinsurance-technology transformation now sit alongside conventional actuarial and claims titles. The hard part is that these skills usually live outside insurance, so hiring managers are pulling candidates from technology and consulting backgrounds into roles that still require deep regulatory and underwriting judgement, and the standards bodies and managing general agents around the carriers are recruiting the same way.

What the coverage shows about where hiring sits

The companies named most often split between large, established carriers and brokers making incremental promotions, and smaller insurtechs and managing general agents announcing a first hire into a newly created role. Underwriting, claims and reinsurance titles dominate, reflecting how much of this activity sits next to product and technology work rather than pure administration, and a change at an industry standards body carries weight beyond its own size because so much of the sector's data exchange depends on it. Moves at brokers and MGAs appear about as often as moves at carriers themselves.

Carrier hires versus broker and vendor hires

A move at a carrier or reinsurer usually concerns underwriting, claims or risk leadership and signals how a balance sheet is being run. A move at a broker concerns distribution and client-facing management instead, and a move at a technology vendor or managing general agent typically concerns sales, platform or compliance leadership, since these firms sell software or capacity rather than carry risk themselves. Reading the employer's business model is usually enough to tell which kind of move a headline describes, even before the new title is spelled out.
