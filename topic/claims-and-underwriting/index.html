Insurance claims and underwriting

691 stories · tracked since Aug 5, 2024 · latest Nov 13, 2026

Underwriting decides which risks an insurer accepts and at what price; claims handling pays out when those risks produce a loss. Together they set an insurer's profit.

How a risk is accepted and a loss is paid

An underwriter reviews a submission from a broker or applicant, assesses the exposure, and decides whether to quote, on what terms and at what price. Pricing draws on actuarial models, loss history and increasingly third-party data. When a loss occurs the insured gives notice, and a handler or adjuster checks that cover applies, investigates, values the loss, and then pays, arranges repair or denies the claim. Large losses can bring in loss adjusters, repair networks and reinsurers.

Why discipline and speed are hard to combine

Underwriting quality and claims cost together decide whether an insurer makes money, so softer pricing or costlier repairs draw immediate attention. The hard parts are information and capacity: submissions arrive as messy documents, losses stay uncertain until settlement, and fraud, litigation and catastrophe events strain handling teams. Regulators also set deadlines and fair-handling rules for responding to policyholders, so automation has to remain explainable, with a person accountable for each decision.

Where the activity sits today

The activity spans large carriers, global brokers, data and analytics providers, specialty underwriting agencies and a long tail of small software firms. The emphasis has moved from digitising intake and forms toward AI that triages submissions, reviews claims evidence and prices risks, with agentic tools now appearing on both sides. Property and catastrophe losses keep claims handling in focus, while specialty and delegated underwriting raise questions of capacity and discipline. The subject often overlaps with partnerships, product launches and funding.
