AI in insurance

1463 stories · tracked since Aug 1, 2024 · latest Nov 4, 2026

AI in insurance is the use of machine learning and language models to price, underwrite, sell and settle insurance, and to cover AI's own risks. Carriers, brokers and software vendors all take part.

How models are used across a policy's life

Insurers apply models at each step of the policy lifecycle. In underwriting they ingest submissions, extract data from documents and score risk. In pricing they fit rates to finer segments. In claims they triage files, flag fraud and read photos and medical records. Generative models add natural-language assistants for agents, adjusters and customers. The parties are carriers, brokers and agents, managing general agents, and software vendors selling core systems and analytics. Terms met elsewhere include straight-through processing, document intelligence, copilots, agentic workflows and model governance.

Why it matters and what makes it hard

Insurance is largely information processing: submissions, policy wording, claims files and correspondence. Models that read and write text therefore reach the core of the work, not its edges. The hard parts are mostly not technical. Pricing and coverage decisions are regulated, so insurers must explain them and show they do not discriminate. Training data is patchy, models can be confidently wrong, and legacy systems slow deployment. Complaint handlers and regulators are also seeing the effects of customers using AI to write long, error-filled submissions.

A tool for insurers, and a risk to be insured

The subject holds two different things that readers often run together. The first is AI as a tool: insurers and brokers using it to run their own operations more cheaply and accurately. The second is AI as a risk: losses caused by AI systems, such as wrong outputs, copyright disputes, liability for autonomous decisions and the physical infrastructure behind it. Existing cyber, professional liability and directors and officers policies were not written with these in mind. Insurers now argue over whether to exclude, clarify or affirmatively cover them, and accumulation across many policyholders is hard to bound.

Who is active and where the emphasis has moved

Activity spans large brokers such as Marsh, Aon, Gallagher and WTW, carriers such as AXA, Chubb and Zurich, and software vendors such as Guidewire, Vertafore and Sapiens. Newer specialty insurers and MGAs such as Corgi, CFC and Cowbell sit alongside them. Early coverage centred on product launches and assistants. More recent items turn to acquisitions of underwriting software, large funding rounds for AI-native insurers, and explicit AI cover. The subject keeps landing in claims and underwriting, funding, partnerships and, through data centres, property and catastrophe risk.
