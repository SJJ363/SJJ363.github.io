Insurance regulation

90 stories · tracked since Aug 5, 2024 · latest Aug 20, 2026

Insurance regulation is the laws and regulators that license insurers, oversee solvency, and approve the rates and policies they sell. In the US that oversight runs state by state rather than through one federal regulator; elsewhere a single national authority usually covers solvency, conduct and now AI use.

Who regulates insurers and how

An insurance regulator is the government body that licenses insurers and brokers, sets how much capital they must hold, and reviews the rates and policy wording before a product reaches customers. In the United States that job sits with each state's insurance department rather than one federal agency; elsewhere a single national authority does it, such as India's IRDAI, Nigeria's NAICOM or the UK's FCA and PRA. Model laws, rate filings, form approval and regulatory sandboxes are the recurring vocabulary of this work.

Why compliance is a moving target now

The friction shows up wherever a new distribution model or underwriting method reaches the desk of a regulator built for a slower industry. A rate change, a new AI-driven pricing model or a policy sold through a retailer's checkout still needs sign-off, and in the US that approval has to be sought state by state rather than once nationally. Regulators are now writing rules for AI use in underwriting and claims, for financial-promotion and conduct standards, and for how much of an insurer's investment risk they are allowed to carry, which makes compliance a moving target rather than a fixed checklist.

Who shows up in this coverage

The companies showing up most often split into two groups: the regulators themselves — Nigeria's NAICOM and India's IRDAI chief among them — and the compliance and brokerage side that has to answer to them, from RegTech platforms such as Kayna, Kwant and Adclear to brokers like Willis Towers Watson, NFP and Lloyd's working through licensing and acquisitions. The coverage increasingly crosses into AI & Automation and Claims & Underwriting, as regulators turn their attention to how insurers use AI, and into Funding and Property & Cat, where new capital and catastrophe exposure both draw regulatory scrutiny.

Solvency rules versus market conduct rules

Two different kinds of oversight get lumped under the same word. Solvency, or prudential, regulation asks whether an insurer holds enough capital to pay future claims; conduct regulation asks whether it is selling and handling those claims fairly, covering advertising, disclosure and complaints handling. Some markets split the two between separate bodies, such as the UK's PRA and FCA; many others, including most US states, hand both to the same commissioner.
