Property and catastrophe insurance

542 stories · tracked since Aug 9, 2024 · latest Oct 30, 2026

Property and catastrophe insurance covers physical assets against damage from fire, storm, flood and quake. The catastrophe layer is the part where one event hits many policies at once.

How property cover and catastrophe risk work

A property policy pays for damage to buildings and contents, and the policyholder pays a premium and carries a deductible. Insurers write many such policies, but one hurricane, wildfire or windstorm can damage thousands at once. To survive that, carriers buy reinsurance, often as excess-of-loss layers or catastrophe bonds sold to capital-markets investors. Brokers place the risk, modelling firms estimate losses, and data providers publish industry loss figures after each event.

Why it is hard to price and keep available

Catastrophe losses are lumpy and correlated, so a run of mild years says little about the next bad one. Climate change, building in exposed places and rising repair costs push models and rates around. Where private carriers retreat, states and governments step in with residual markets or pools, such as flood schemes, and homeowners face higher premiums or thinner cover. Surplus lines carriers fill the gap for risks standard insurers decline.

What the activity below shows

Activity centres on brokers and reinsurers rather than startups: Aon, Howden, Gallagher, Marsh McLennan Agency and Swiss Re appear often alongside data and analytics providers such as Verisk, CatIQ and AM Best. After each major storm, loss estimates arrive from modelling firms and industry data providers. Alongside that are launches of new specialty carriers and facilities, and appointments in reinsurance broking.

Where technology is being applied

Newer entrants mostly sell tools rather than balance-sheet cover: wildfire risk models, property-level analytics, claims platforms for homeowners and AI workspaces for underwriting and reinsurance. Managing agencies and carriers adopt these to sharpen exposure analysis. There is also experimentation with new structures, such as parametric cover, transactions that combine property catastrophe with cyber risk, and facilities spanning several lines. The same stories often fall under product launches, partnerships and claims.
