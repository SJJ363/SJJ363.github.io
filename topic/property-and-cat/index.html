Property and catastrophe insurance

196 stories · tracked since Aug 9, 2024 · latest Aug 7, 2026

Property and catastrophe insurance covers physical damage to buildings and contents, including losses from major natural disasters. The catastrophe side deals with correlated, large-scale peril risk that insurers spread through reinsurance and capital markets rather than absorb alone.

How a claim moves from a homeowner to global capital

A primary insurer sells a policy on a building and its contents and prices the premium to the peril involved — wind, fire, flood, earthquake. When a loss is large enough to threaten many policies at once, the insurer cedes part of that risk to a reinsurer, who may pass a further slice through retrocession or into the capital markets as a catastrophe bond. A parametric policy skips the claims process, paying out once a measured trigger, such as wind speed or an earthquake's magnitude, crosses an agreed threshold, rather than waiting on an adjuster to assess the damage.

Why the pricing keeps getting harder

The central difficulty is correlation: an earthquake or hurricane does not damage one policyholder, it damages thousands at once, so insurers cannot simply average losses across a large enough pool the way they can with car crashes or burglaries. Wildfire and flood mapping has grown more precise, but the underlying risk keeps shifting as weather patterns change and as development pushes further into fire- and flood-prone land, so a model built on last decade's losses can understate this decade's. In the costliest regions, insurers have pulled back or raised prices sharply enough that governments have stepped in with backstop programs to keep coverage available at all.

Who is active and where the work is going

The coverage spans large reinsurers and carriers — Swiss Re, Munich Re, Arch, Chubb, Liberty Mutual — alongside newer, narrower entrants: parametric specialists building index-based wildfire and flood cover, and catastrophe-modeling and geospatial-analytics firms feeding faster damage estimates into claims and underwriting. A recurring thread is data and automation reshaping how a loss gets assessed and priced, and consolidation as larger analytics and data firms acquire the specialists that built this capability. The activity overlaps most with claims and underwriting, funding rounds for the newer entrants, and product launches for parametric and AI-driven tools.

Property insurance versus catastrophe reinsurance

The two terms describe adjacent but distinct layers. Property insurance is the retail-facing policy a homeowner or business buys to cover its own building and contents against everyday and large-scale perils alike. Catastrophe reinsurance and catastrophe bonds sit behind that, insuring the insurer against the tail risk of many claims arriving simultaneously; buyers of that protection are insurance and reinsurance companies, not individual policyholders. A parametric product can appear at either layer — sold directly to a business or government, or used by an insurer to manage its own concentrated exposure.
