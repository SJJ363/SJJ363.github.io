Funding

554 stories · tracked since Aug 6, 2024 · latest Jul 31, 2026

Funding is the capital insurance-technology companies raise from investors, in exchange for equity, debt or a share of future revenue. Rounds run from small seed checks to large late-stage raises, and are often labeled Series A, B, C and beyond as a company matures.

An investor, usually a venture or growth-equity fund and sometimes an insurer investing strategically, wires cash to the company in return for newly issued shares. A term sheet sets the valuation, how much of the company is sold, and what control the investor gets, such as a board seat or a say in a future sale.

It matters because building underwriting models, claims automation or a distribution platform takes years of losses before it pays for itself, and insurance itself is capital-intensive and regulated in ways that make outside capital harder to deploy directly into risk-bearing. Much of what gets funded here is the technology and brokerage layer around insurance rather than an insurer's own balance sheet.

Coverage here spans far beyond any one market, with rounds landing in Indonesia, Nigeria, the Balkans, Egypt and France alongside the more familiar US and UK names, and it sits most often alongside AI & Automation and Health & Life, as claims and underwriting tools draw much of the recent attention. Some companies return for a second or third round within a short stretch as their valuation climbs.
