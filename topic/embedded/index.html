Embedded insurance

118 stories · tracked since Aug 8, 2024 · latest Aug 4, 2026

Embedded insurance is cover sold inside another company's product or checkout, bought from that company rather than from an insurer directly. An insurer or MGA underwrites behind the scenes while the host brand supplies the customer, the moment of sale, and often the interface.

How the policy actually reaches the checkout

An insurer or managing general agent builds the product and carries the underwriting risk, then exposes it through an API that a distribution partner - a retailer, bank, airline, car marketplace or software platform - plugs into its own checkout or app. The partner supplies the customer relationship and the moment of sale; the insurer or MGA supplies the paper, the pricing and the claims handling behind it. The policy is usually bundled with or attached to a purchase already underway rather than sold as a standalone decision, which is what separates it from a comparison site or a broker's storefront.

Why insurers are chasing this distribution route

The appeal for an insurer or MGA is reach without building a brand or running its own acquisition funnel: the retailer, lender or platform already has the customer's attention at the moment a related risk becomes obvious, whether that is a phone, a rental car, a shipment or a subcontractor's tools. The hard part sits on the technology and compliance side rather than the product side: underwriting has to run in real time inside someone else's flow, pricing has to update from data the partner holds, and every jurisdiction the partner operates in brings its own rules about who may sell insurance at the point of sale.

Who is active and how the mix has shifted

The companies most visible here split between distribution platforms that connect insurers to partners - plugging cover into banking apps, freight bookings, short-term rental listings and gig-economy apps - and the carriers and brokers supplying the underwriting behind those connections. Recent activity leans toward established insurers and technology firms partnering with specialist embedded platforms on narrow, occupational programs, such as construction subcontractors or delivery robots, rather than the broad consumer point-of-sale deals that once defined the category. Consolidation has picked up too, with platforms acquiring bancassurance capability rather than building it themselves.

Embedded insurance versus affinity and bancassurance

Affinity insurance and bancassurance are older cousins of the same idea - a bank or membership group co-brands and cross-sells a policy - but the sale still happens as a separate step, often after a call or a follow-up email. What marks a program as embedded rather than affinity is that the purchase happens inside the same transaction as the underlying product, priced and bound automatically with no human handoff. In practice the two blur: several bank and retailer partnerships in this category are described both ways, and a bancassurance platform that adds real-time binding is, functionally, becoming an embedded one.
