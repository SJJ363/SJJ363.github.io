Embedded insurance

176 stories · tracked since Aug 8, 2024 · latest Oct 28, 2026

Embedded insurance is cover sold inside another company's product or checkout, so customers buy it without visiting an insurer. The host brand owns the customer; an insurer or MGA carries the risk.

How a policy gets sold inside someone else's product

Three parties are involved. The host is the retailer, bank, airline, car dealer or software platform that already has the customer. The risk carrier or managing general agent writes the policy and holds the capacity. Between them sits technology: an API or middle-office platform that quotes, binds, issues and services the policy inside the host's own flow. The host usually earns a commission or fee, and the customer sees one optional line at checkout, often priced from data the host already holds.

Why insurers want it and what makes it hard

Insurers reach customers at the moment of need without paying to acquire them one at a time, and hosts gain a revenue line and, they argue, better engagement. The difficulties are structural. Policies are small, so margins are thin. Capacity must be stable, since a carrier that withdraws strands every partner built on it. Rules on selling insurance through a non-insurance firm vary by country. Claims handling must feel as seamless as the sale.

What the activity looks like across the archive

The recurring names are a mix: specialist embedded platforms such as Cover Genius and Slingshot, technology providers such as InsureMO, and large carriers and brokers including Aviva, Chubb and Willis Towers Watson. Car sales and electric vehicles appear through Carvana and Root, and travel recurs often. Over the period the emphasis has moved from announcing partnerships toward funding, consolidation and carrier capacity, with platforms buying European peers and an insurer rescuing a carrier that supported embedded partners.

Neighbouring subjects it keeps landing in

Embedded cover overlaps with several other topics. Partnerships are the commonest form, since each deal pairs a host with a carrier. Funding follows, as platform builders raise capital to supply the infrastructure. AI and automation appear where underwriting and pricing are tuned to checkout data, and M&A where platforms and carriers combine to secure capacity or reach new regions. Embedded finance is the wider idea that insurance sits within.
