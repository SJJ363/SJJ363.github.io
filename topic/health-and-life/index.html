Health and life insurance

314 stories · tracked since Aug 5, 2024 · latest Oct 30, 2026

Health and life insurance covers medical costs or pays a sum on death or disability, sold directly, through employers, or via brokers and agents. The two lines are often bundled together in employee benefits packages even though they are priced and regulated separately.

How a policy is priced, sold and paid out

Life insurance pays a lump sum on death or a defined disability event; health insurance reimburses medical costs or pays cash benefits during treatment. Carriers set premiums using actuarial mortality and morbidity tables, then distribute through brokers, agents, employer group plans, or embedded partnerships inside another company's product. A policy administration system holds the contract, a third-party administrator or claims platform processes what is owed, and a broker or benefits consultant usually sits between the carrier and the person buying cover.

What makes underwriting and distribution hard

Health and life underwriting depends on medical and lifestyle data scattered across providers, pharmacies and prior insurers, so carriers and brokers spend heavily on connecting records rather than pricing risk. Distribution is layered too: an employer chooses a benefits plan, a broker negotiates it, and an employee picks among options during a short enrollment window, which makes onboarding speed and clear plan comparison as important as underwriting. Claims handling for chronic and long-tail health conditions adds another layer of manual review that automation has only begun to touch.

What the activity below shows

Coverage here mixes established carriers such as Ageas Federal Life, SBI Life and FWD Life with dedicated health insurers such as Jubilee and Galaxy Health Insurance, and a layer of technology vendors, from policy platforms like Duck Creek and Vertafore to distribution tools like Luma and iPipeline, that sell into both. Much of the recent activity is partnership and embedded distribution, a health insurer teaming with a digital platform to reach new customers, alongside leadership moves at established carriers. The nearest neighbouring subjects are funding, partnerships, and claims and underwriting, reflecting how often a health or life story is really a distribution story.

How health and life differ by market

The two lines diverge by geography as much as by product. In the United States and Canada, life insurance is largely sold through agents and employer group benefits, with health insurance bound up in a separate, heavily regulated employer and government system. In India, life insurers such as Aditya Birla Sun Life, Bajaj Life and SBI Life sell retail savings-linked policies alongside pure protection, while health cover moves increasingly through digital brokers and embedded partnerships. In Africa, health insurers such as Jubilee lean on mobile and embedded channels to reach customers carriers could not otherwise afford to serve.
