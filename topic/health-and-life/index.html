Topic

Health and life insurance

335 stories · tracked since Aug 5, 2024 · latest Nov 12, 2026

Health and life insurance covers medical costs, or pays a sum to named beneficiaries when the policyholder dies. Insurtechs work on online sales, underwriting and claims, while carriers, brokers and banks use digital channels and partnerships to reach buyers.

How health and life policies work

Health insurance reimburses or directly pays for medical care, either through a state scheme, an employer's group plan or a policy bought by an individual. Life insurance pays a benefit to beneficiaries on death, in term, whole-life or annuity form. Underwriters price each by assessing medical history and risk, and policies are sold by agents, brokers, banks, employers and, increasingly, online. Premium, claim, beneficiary and underwriting are the core vocabulary.

Why it matters and what is hard

Both lines are large, regulated and slow to change, so small improvements in sales or claims handling carry real weight. The hard parts are underwriting without long medical questionnaires, keeping customers who rarely touch their policy until a claim, and handling sensitive health data. Mis-selling and online scams are a constant concern, and regulators in some markets are reshaping how policies are distributed. Public attitudes to automated decisions on care and cover remain wary.

Where the activity sits today

Activity is spread across digital insurers selling health cover directly, life insurers building instant online applications, and established carriers partnering with distributors or insurtechs to reach new buyers. Partnerships and distribution deals recur more often than new product invention, and acquisitions of agencies and planning firms show consolidation among sellers. AI features in underwriting and customer service, and emerging markets such as India, Bangladesh, Egypt and Kenya appear alongside the US and Europe, where small health-access startups also raise funding.

How health and life differ

The two lines insure different things. Health insurance is high-frequency: claims arrive often, provider networks matter and costs are managed continuously. Life insurance is low-frequency and long-dated, with a single large payout and, in annuities and retirement products, a savings element. That is why health players tend to focus on access and claims, and life players on application speed and distribution. Readers often conflate them because many insurers and agencies sell both.
