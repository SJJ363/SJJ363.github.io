Insurance product launches

1096 stories · tracked since Aug 7, 2024 · latest Nov 27, 2026

Insurance product launches are the introduction of a new policy, program, platform or entry into a new market by an insurer, broker or insurtech. They range from a new cover to a tool sold to carriers.

What counts as a launch and who makes it

A launch is the point at which a carrier, managing general agent, broker or technology vendor makes something available that did not exist before. It may be a new line of cover, a program written on a carrier's paper, an existing product opened in a new state or country, or software such as an underwriting or submission-intake tool. Regulators often sit in the path: a new carrier needs a licence, and new rates or policy forms usually need filing before they can be sold.

Why launches matter and what makes them hard

Launches are how insurers find growth outside their existing book, and how newer entrants test whether a niche can be underwritten profitably. The difficulty is that a new product has no loss history. Pricing rests on analogues, modelling and judgement, and a wrong guess only shows up in claims years later. Capacity must also be lined up from reinsurers or fronting carriers, and distribution has to be built, because a product nobody sells earns nothing.

Where launch activity sits today

Much of the activity comes from large brokers and carriers rather than startups alone. Brokers such as Aon and Marsh announce programs and exchanges for specific risks, data centers being a recurring example, while carriers like Aviva, Zurich and AXA release digital channels and updated products. Insurtechs such as Root, Cowbell, Stand and Corgi tend to launch by entering new states or opening a new kind of carrier.

How the emphasis has shifted toward AI and new risks

Launches increasingly arrive as AI tools for underwriting, submission intake and policyholder advice, alongside covers for risks that barely existed before, such as parametric protection for computing hardware or digital infrastructure. Telematics-based auto, pay-per-use mobility cover and cyber products keep appearing. These launches overlap with automation, claims and underwriting, property and catastrophe risk, and partnership announcements.
